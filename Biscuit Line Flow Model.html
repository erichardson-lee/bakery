Stations

The checkboxes apply straight away. Rates, batch sizes and process times apply when you press Apply and restart. A station's rate is its batch size × 60 ÷ its process time; for the cooling rack the batch size is the number of slots.

Assumptions

  • Store cupboard releases mix into the Mixer's queue at the set rate. Blank means no limit.
  • Sale rate caps how many trays can be sold per hour. Boxed trays go to Ready to ship and are sold from there; trays made above the sale rate wait there unsold and earn nothing, but still cost £1 each to start. Selling capacity that goes unused because nothing was ready (for example while the line is filling up) is not saved up for later. Blank means no limit: every boxed tray sells straight away.
  • Money: £1 is spent for each tray started at the Mixer, and £1.50 is earned for each tray sold. Trays boxed but not yet sold earn nothing.
  • Batch sizes and process times below are the defaults and can be changed in Settings.
  • Mixer needs a full batch of mix to start (1 tray's worth by default). Each batch takes 5 minutes and goes to the Tray-er queue when finished.
  • Tray-er takes 1 minute per batch of 1 tray.
  • Oven bakes batches of 4 trays, takes 20 minutes per batch and locks while cooking. By default it waits for a full batch; untick the checkbox to start with whatever is waiting.
  • Cooling rack takes 30 minutes per tray and has 6 slots. Its batch size is the number of slots.
  • Busy % is the share of time a station's slots were in use. With "Busy % from each station's first item" ticked, it only counts from the minute the station first had anything waiting or in progress, so the wait for the first item to reach it at the start is left out. Before then it shows "—". After Finish day, each station stops counting once it has processed its last item and nothing more can reach it, so the idle time at the end of the day is left out too.
  • Boxer takes 1 minute per batch of 1 tray.
  • Ready to ship counts boxed trays waiting to be sold; Sold counts trays that have been sold.
  • Lead time, new tray: how long (h:mm) a tray whose mix joined the back of the Mixer's queue now would take to be boxed (ready to ship, not sold). It runs a copy of the line forward from now, first in, first out, with normal cycle times even when random cycle times are on. It shows "—" after Finish day and ">7 days" if the tray would not be boxed within a week.
  • Avg lead time: the average time (h:mm, to the nearest minute) from a tray's mix being released into the Mixer's queue to that tray being boxed, over every tray boxed so far. Trays are matched first in, first out. It shows "—" until the first tray is boxed.
  • Finish day stops the store cupboard releasing mix and the Mixer starting new batches, then runs until no trays are left on the line. While finishing, the oven bakes part batches so it doesn't wait for trays that will never come. Reset starts a new day.
  • Rework (turn on its buttons in Settings), on each station, means the work in progress there has to be done again: the batch restarts its cycle from zero (a new cycle time is drawn if random cycle times are on). On the cooling rack it restarts the one tray closest to done. Nothing is lost and it costs no extra money; it just takes longer and keeps the station busy.
  • Random cycle times: each cycle is shorter by the set % (25%), normal (50%) or longer by the set % (25%). The change is rounded to whole minutes and cycles never go below 1 minute. Stations with short cycles, such as the Tray-er and Boxer, cannot vary by a fraction of a minute.
